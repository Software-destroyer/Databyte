SLIDE 2: 1. INTRODUCTION & BACKGROUND
[Visual: Left: NIDS background bullets; Right top: Red warning box;
 Right bottom: Comparison diagram showing Same-Dataset 99% vs Cross-Dataset 40-60%]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[SPEAKER 1 — Pynskhemlang]:
"To establish context, Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS) are the critical
perimeter defense guarding enterprise and national infrastructure.
Historically, systems relied on signature-based tools such as Snort, Suricata,
and Zeek. While precise, they suffer from structural obsolescence: they cannot
detect zero-day exploits, evasive polymorphic malware, or emerging threat variants.

Consequently, academia and industry transitioned heavily toward Machine Learning
and Deep Learning classifiers. In published academic papers, models like Decision
Trees, Random Forests, SVM, and XGBoost routinely claim 98% to 99.5% accuracy on
public benchmarks like CICIDS2017 and UNSW-NB15.

However, as shown in the highlighted warning and diagram on the slide, this
apparent perfection is an operational illusion — what we identify as the
'Same-Dataset Evaluation Illusion.'

In standard research setups, models are trained and tested on identically
partitioned 80/20 splits of a single dataset. Because training and testing traffic
share identical network topologies, packet capture tools, operating systems, and
background noise, classifiers memorize dataset-specific artifacts.

When that same model is tested zero-shot on an independent dataset — like a model
trained on CICIDS transferred onto UNSW-NB15 — detection accuracy catastrophically
collapses down to 40% to 60%, with False Alarm Rates skyrocketing. Our project is
dedicated to breaking this illusion and engineering real solutions."

SLIDE 3: 2. RESEARCH GAPS & MOTIVATION
[Visual: 3 Gap Cards (Diagnosis without Remediation, Feature Ontology Mismatch,
 No Bidirectional Benchmarks) + Bottom Solution Architecture Pipeline Diagram]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[SPEAKER 2 — Tehjib Almas Junaid]:
"Thank you, Pynskhemlang. Now, one might ask: Hasn't cross-dataset evaluation
already been studied in academic literature?

The answer is: Prior literature has diagnosed the disease, but nobody has
engineered the cure. Our literature review identified three distinct research gaps:

First, 'Diagnosis Without Remediation.' Foundational studies — such as Verkerken
et al. (2022) in the Journal of Network and Systems Management, Cantone et al.
(2024) at IEEE CSR, and Rosa et al. (2022) — observed that cross-dataset transfer
causes accuracy to plummet by 25% to 42%, and they stated that feature harmonization
is mandatory. But they stopped right there! They treated the drop as an insurmountable
dead-end and did NOT engineer domain adaptation or feature alignment mechanisms.

Second, 'Semantic Feature Ontology Mismatch.' The two premier global benchmarks —
CICIDS2017, extracted via CICFlowMeter with 80+ features, and UNSW-NB15, extracted
via Argus/Zeek with 49 features — have entirely different naming schemas, measurement
units, and granularities. Prior studies either stayed within the same family (CIC to
CIC) or arbitrarily threw away 90% of features down to 5 naive columns.

Third, 'Absence of Bidirectional Benchmarks.' Prior studies test unidirectional
transfer (A to B) and omit the reverse direction (B to A). Furthermore, the relative
resilience of tree ensembles versus margin-maximizing SVMs under network covariate
shift remains systematically unranked.

As visualized in our solution pipeline at the bottom of the slide: We take CICIDS2017
and UNSW-NB15, establish a unified Semantic Feature Alignment Ontology, execute
bidirectional transfer, apply CORAL domain adaptation and feature selection, and
actively bridge this generalization gap."

SLIDE 8: 7. DOMAIN ALIGNMENT & MITIGATION STRATEGIES
[Visual: 3 Mitigation Cards (Feature Selection, Normalization, CORAL Alignment)
 + Bottom: 3 Statistical Divergence Metric Cards (KL Divergence, Wasserstein, MMD)]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[SPEAKER 3 — Bamelaai Marbaniang]:
"This slide represents the core technical remediation engineered in our project.

To bridge the domain divide, we employ three complementary interventions:
First, Statistical Feature Selection: Using Mutual Information (MI) to quantify
non-linear dependency between features and attack labels across both corpora, and
Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE) to prune environment-overfitted variables.

Second, Domain-Invariant Normalization: Network link speeds differ fundamentally —
UNSW's testbed operated at 10 Gbps, whereas CICIDS ran on 1 Gbps, producing orders-
of-magnitude discrepancies in raw byte counts. We apply Quantile Transformation
and Robust Z-score scaling to eliminate magnitude distortions without destroying
the underlying distribution shapes.

Third, Correlation Alignment (CORAL): This is an unsupervised domain adaptation
algorithm. CORAL computes the covariance matrices of the source and target datasets,
whitens the source domain, and re-colors it with the target domain covariance:
  X_aligned = X_source * (C_source)^(-1/2) * (C_target)^(1/2)
The key advantage of CORAL is that it requires ZERO labels from the target network!

At the bottom of the slide, we evaluate why this works through three rigorous
divergence metrics:
  • KL Divergence: Measuring relative entropy between probability distributions.
  • Wasserstein Distance: Earth Mover's Distance calculating the cost of transforming
    the flow manifold.
  • Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD): Testing distribution discrepancy in Reproducing
    Kernel Hilbert Space."

QUESTION 1:
"How is your project different from Verkerken et al. (2022) or Cantone et al. (2024)?
Haven't they already evaluated CICIDS2017 and UNSW-NB15?"

ANSWER:
"Sir/Madam, that is an essential distinction.
Verkerken et al. (2022) and Cantone et al. (2024) did what we call 'Observational
Diagnosis' — they performed cross-dataset testing, proved that accuracy plummets by
25% to 42%, and concluded in their discussion that 'feature harmonization is mandatory.'
However, they stopped there. They treated the collapse as an insurmountable dead-end
and did not engineer a solution.

Our project picks up exactly where Verkerken and Cantone stopped:
1. We actively BUILD the semantic feature mapping ontology they called for.
2. We implement ACTIVE REMEDIATION using CORAL (Correlation Alignment) and
   feature selection (Mutual Information & RFE) to close the gap.
3. We quantify the recovery using a formal metric: Generalization Recovery Ratio (GRR).
4. We build an interactive Streamlit tool for security practitioners.
In short: Verkerken diagnosed the disease; our project engineers the treatment."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
QUESTION 2:
"Why did you choose CORAL (Correlation Alignment) instead of Deep Learning Domain
Adaptation like DANN (Domain Adversarial Neural Networks)?"

ANSWER:
"We chose CORAL for three engineering reasons:
1. Unsupervised & Label-Free: In real network deployment, target network traffic is
   unlabeled. CORAL aligns second-order statistics (covariance matrices) without
   requiring any target labels.
2. Computational Efficiency: CORAL is a closed-form linear algebraic transformation.
   It computes in seconds on standard CPU hardware, whereas adversarial neural networks
   (like DANN) require hours of GPU training and suffer from minimax instability.
3. Model-Agnostic: CORAL transforms the feature space itself, meaning the aligned
   features can immediately feed into tree ensembles like XGBoost and Random Forest,
   which are the highest-performing models on tabular flow data."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
QUESTION 3:
"Why do you use Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) instead of just Accuracy?"

ANSWER:
"Network intrusion datasets exhibit severe class imbalance — over 80% to 85% of flows
are benign. If a naive model simply predicts 'Benign' for every single packet, it would
achieve 85% accuracy while missing 100% of cyber attacks!
Accuracy is completely deceptive in cybersecurity. MCC incorporates all four quadrants
of the confusion matrix (TP, TN, FP, FN) and produces a balanced score between -1 and +1,
where 0 represents random guessing. An MCC collapse from +0.98 to +0.10 accurately reflects
the total failure of attack detection."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
QUESTION 4:
"What happens if domain alignment still doesn't recover 100% of the accuracy?"

ANSWER:
"That is an expected and scientifically honest outcome. A 100% recovery across completely
different physical networks with different attack tools is theoretically impossible
due to concept drift. What our project proves is how much of the deficit can be recovered
through feature harmonization and covariance alignment. Even moving cross-dataset accuracy
from 45% to 75% or 80% represents a monumental operational victory for production NIDS."
